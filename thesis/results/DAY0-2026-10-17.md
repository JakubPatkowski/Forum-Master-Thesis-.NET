## 0. ERRATUM (added 2026-10-17 evening, after the fairness review) — read before quoting anything below

Three statements in this Day-0 report turned out to be wrong; the numbers stay as archived (they are what was
measured), but their interpretation changes. The aligned procedure that fixes all three is harness v1 +
`scripts/ab-round.sh`; see `thesis/results/AB-COMPARISON-v1.md`.

1. **"Journeys / s: A 73.57 vs B 76.36" is NOT a throughput difference.** The journey COUNTS were practically
   identical (run 1: A 38 599, B 38 611 — a closed model with fixed VUs and think time). k6's `rate` divides by
   the whole test duration *including setup*, and A's setup was ~20 s longer (200 Argon2id logins vs PBKDF2):
   A 524.6 s vs B 504.7 s. Over the 420 s scenario both sides served ≈ 91.9 journeys/s.
2. **The CPU means include the setup phase.** Recomputed over the last 420 s of each run (scenario only, same
   samples): A ≈ 416 m vs B ≈ 363 m (ratio ≈ 1.15) instead of 372 m vs 306 m (ratio ≈ 1.22). Direction unchanged,
   magnitude overstated.
3. **Deviation 3 ("B gzip in-process ON → B paid compression CPU") is wrong.** k6 sends NO `Accept-Encoding`
   header by default (verified on k6 v2.1.0), so neither B's middleware nor the ingress compressed anything on
   either side during Day-0. Harness v1 sends `Accept-Encoding: gzip` on every request (browser behaviour).

Further Day-0 biases found in the same review (all fixed in v1): B's J6 words came from sentences B-09 removed
(would have returned ~0 results after B-09 — on Day-0 they matched B's old sentences, see deviation 6); A's
search returned private-category threads (≈1 580 rows ranked vs B's public-only — A-12, fixed); different thread
pools (A newest 2×50, B newest 2×20 per space → B opened threads with ~35 % more comments); ~half of A's J5 calls
were idempotent no-ops (no write) while every B vote wrote.

## 4. Deviations in THIS run (must accompany every quoted number)
1. **B page size 8 (feed) / 10 (search) vs A 20** — B-01 not yet applied. B rendered less than half the items
   per J1/J6; B's J1/J6 latency and CPU are therefore **optimistic**, A's `req/s` and payload are not comparable.
2. **B friend fan-out on thread create was ON** (B-02 not applied) — irrelevant for this mix (J4 weight 0).
3. **B gzip in-process ON** (B-04 not applied) → B paid compression CPU inside the measured pods; A did not
   (ingress). B's CPU is therefore **pessimistic** by the gzip cost.
4. **B pgx pool default 8 per pod** (B-05a not applied) vs A 30 — not saturated at 80 VU.
5. **B seed shape** (volumes equal): 1 pinned vs 16; comments 25 %-on-1 % vs squared-uniform; 12000 votes incl.
   10 % downvotes + 3000 likes vs 11250/3750 likes; 10 fixed sentences 0.3–1.2 KB vs 31-word bank 120–320 words;
   2 private spaces × ~200 members vs 4 × 25; no blocked/deleted rows on B.
6. **Search selectivity**: both sides' word lists hit nearly every row (B `ILIKE %word%` over 10 sentences;
   A FTS over a 31-word bank) → J6 measures "match everything then sort/rank 1600 rows" on both (OD-6).
7. Write mix grew the datasets during the runs (comments 9000→9050/9073, reactions 15000→15033/15083 at the
   start of each side's run; symmetric procedure).
8. A's HTTP calls per journey (2–3) vs B's (1): paradigm, by design.
9. A's frontend pod (SSR counterpart) was scaled to 0 during B's run and to 1 otherwise; it is not in the
   "app pods" rows above (those count the 2 API pods on A and the 2 gomx pods on B).
